A seeded hard-sphere stepper inside `Sim` is deterministic by construction and needs no crate. The advisor agreed. ## First slice: projectile, pendulum, spring

- **Shared pieces:**
  - **`motion::Rng`** is SplitMix64 with `next_u64`, `unit` (in (0, 1]) and `range`. It was lifted from `math::normal_samples`, which now uses it with the same bits, so the snapshots are unchanged. It is there so `physics` and `chemistry` do not depend on `math`.
  - **`kits::short`**, the number formatter, moved up from `kits::math` for the same reason.
- **Feature `physics`:**
  - `fframes_kit::kits::physics`, with prelude exports behind the feature. It adds no crates.
  - `docs/components/physics.md` is doctested when `physics` is on, and the gallery enables it.
- **Conventions** (written in the module docs and at the top of `physics.md`):
  - figures are placed in units of `ui.space`, with `scale` units per meter;
  - quantities are SI, and `GRAVITY` is 9.81;
  - each figure has its own clock: local time minus `delay`, times `time_scale`;
  - the fixed parts draw with `enter`, the moving parts fade with `parts_enter`;
  - a figure whose motion data is missing draws nothing.
- **Simulations:** RK4 (`physics::rk4`), 240 steps a second, stored in a `Sim` that is built once. Explicit Euler would gain energy and fail the pendulum's conservation test.
- **Figures:**
  1. **`Projectile`** is a closed form, so it has no `Sim`. It draws:
     - a dashed trace behind the ball;
     - the velocity and its two components. They fade out over 0.25 s after the landing, because a landed ball is at rest;
     - the peak with a dashed drop to the ground and `h = … m`, and `R = … m` under the landing point. Both fade in when they are reached, which keeps `--pops` quiet.

     `flight_time`, `position`, `velocity`, `apex` and `range` are public. A ball thrown flat or downward never flies.
  2. **`Pendulum` with `EnergyBars`:**
     - `PendulumMotion::new(length, angle0, seconds)`, or `::damped(length, angle0, damping, seconds)`, is `θ'' = -g/L sin θ - c θ'`. Its methods are `state`, `energy(t, mass)` (the potential is 0 at the lowest point), `amplitude` and `small_angle_period`.
     - The pendulum draws a ceiling, a dashed rest line, a dashed arc of the swing's reach, the rod, the bob, and an angle arc with its value in degrees.
     - The value sits on the bisector of the angle, so it moves with the angle. The first version anchored it left or right of the rest line, and `--pops` flagged the jump at 0°. At small angles the value now touches the rod.
     - `EnergyBars` takes plain values: kinetic, potential and their sum, the sum stacked from the two colours. You feed it `motion.energy(t, mass)` at the figure's clock. Zero-height bars are left out, so the renderer does not warn about them.
  3. **`SpringMass`:**
     - `SpringMotion::new(stiffness, mass, x0, seconds)` / `::damped(..)` is `m x'' = -k x - c x'`. Its methods are `state`, `energy`, `period` and `trace(until, per_second)`.
     - It draws a wall, a floor, a dashed rest line, a zigzag spring and the block.
     - With `plot: Some(axes.map(ui))` it draws the displacement so far as a `LinePlot` on those axes, with a dot at the current value.
- **Gallery** (`scenes/physics.rs`):
  - `Projectile`: 11 m/s at 0.95 rad, 3.5 s;
  - `Pendulum`: 2.6 m from 0.9 rad, with the bars, 6 s;
  - `SpringMass`: k 20, m 1, c 0.6, 5.5 s. The scene ends when the plot's 5 s of axes end; at 6 s the block ran on past the last plotted point.

## Second slice: wave, free body, orbit, field lines

- **`Wave`** is a closed form with four kinds (`WaveKind`):
  - transverse, and longitudinal (a row of particle lines);
  - standing, with its nodes and dashed envelope;
  - superposition, the two waves thin and their sum thick.

  With `front` the medium starts at rest and the wave leaves the left end, with no jump at the front. `marker` picks out one point of the medium.
- **`FreeBody`** takes `Force { name, x, y, color }` values in newtons:
  - the arrows grow in turn and their labels ride the tips;
  - then the forces dim to half and the net force grows dashed, labelled beside its tip, so it stays off forces on the same line.

  The gallery font has no `Σ`, so the label is `net = 4 N`, and `net_name` renames it.
- **`Orbit`** solves Kepler's equation by Newton's method for each frame (`eccentric_anomaly`, which converges up to e = 0.99). The first orbit shades `sweeps` sectors, each swept in the same time.
- **`Field`** traces the field lines of point charges once, with midpoint steps of 0.02 units:
  - lines leave the positive charges and end on a negative charge's centre or at `bounds`;
  - with only negative charges, the lines come in from `bounds` instead.

  `FieldLines` draws the lines on together, each with an arrowhead at its middle, then a test charge follows one line.
- **Gallery:** `Wave` (the four kinds in rows), `FreeBody`, `Orbit` (e = 0.6, 3 sweeps) and `FieldLines` (a dipole, 16 lines).

## Third slice: circuit, ray diagram, collision

- **`Circuit`** is a series loop: battery left, switch top, bulb right, resistor bottom. Each part can be left out, and the wire runs on.
  - The switch's lever closes over 0.3 s at `on_at`.
  - Then dots flow clockwise out of the long plate (+) at `current_speed` pixels a second, skipping the parts, and the bulb glows with a halo.
  - The resistor reuses the spring's zigzag, now in `physics::zigzag`.
- **`RayDiagram`** draws the three principal rays of a thin lens or a spherical mirror (`Optic`). The sign of `focal` picks converging or diverging, concave or convex.
  - The rays draw on in turn, then the image forms from `1/d_o + 1/d_i = 1/f`.
  - A virtual image is dashed with the rays' backward extensions.
  - A mirror is the lens with the outgoing rays turned back.
- **`Refraction`** is the plan's "refraction" part of the ray diagram, as a sibling component:
  - Snell's law with the weak reflection and the two angles;
  - past the critical angle, total internal reflection.
- **`Collision1D`** is a closed form with a coefficient of restitution:
  - heavier carts are longer;
  - velocity arrows on the carts;
  - momentum bars for A, B and the stacked total.

  A cart stops at the end of the track. The gallery scene ends before cart B gets there, because the track's push would change the total.
- **Gallery:** `Circuit`, `RayDiagram` (a lens), `Mirror`, `Refraction` and `Collision1D`. The labels of all three components fade with `parts_enter`. The first strips drew them before the lines.
